Help & Architecture

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about CivicLens AI triage, multilingual intake, and automated department routing.

How does CivicLens process complaints in Indian regional languages?
CivicLens features native script and phonetic detection for Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, and English. When a citizen submits a complaint in their regional language (or uses voice input), our natural language engine normalizes the text into canonical English for municipal engineers while preserving original vernacular terms and location landmarks.
How does the AI determine severity and the mandatory 1-line reason?
Severity is calculated based on life-safety hazard, infrastructure vulnerability, and geographic context (such as proximity to primary schools, hospitals, or transit corridors). Every ticket receives an automated rating (Critical, High, Medium, or Low) along with a transparent one-line justification explaining why that priority was assigned.
How does spatial deduplication prevent duplicate crew dispatch?
When multiple residents report the same incident (e.g., an overflowing garbage bin or broken water main on 12th Main), CivicLens compares geographic coordinates and semantic similarity. Instead of dispatching separate contractors, subsequent reports are automatically clustered as duplicate endorsements under the primary ticket.
Why is user sign-in required before posting a complaint?
Requiring citizen authentication eliminates malicious automated spam, allows citizens to monitor resolution status in real time, and enables automated WhatsApp dispatch notifications with verifiable SLA tracking. Citizens can test the flow using the 1-click demo account citizen@demo.in.
Which municipal departments are integrated for automated routing?
CivicLens routes tickets to 6 core urban divisions: Roads & Infrastructure (PWD), Electricity & Streetlighting (BESCOM / State DISCOM), Water Supply & Sewage (BWSSB), Solid Waste Management (SWM), Drainage & Flood Control, and Urban Forestry / Horticulture.
What are the response SLAs for Critical life-safety hazards?
Critical emergencies (such as sparking exposed wires, open manholes near schools, or major water main bursts) are flagged with immediate 4-hour SLA targets and high-visibility alerts on the Officer Command Center. High, Medium, and Low severity issues follow 24-hour, 48-hour, and 72-hour municipal schedules.

Triage & Routing Architecture

  • 1. Language Detection & Translation: Ingests regional text and provides an accurate English canonical version for city engineers.
  • 2. Domain Classification: Automatically classifies into 6 divisions: Roads, Electricity, Water, Waste, Drainage, or Trees.
  • 3. Severity Scoring + 1-Line Reason: Analyzes proximity hazards (schools, hospitals, open water) to assign Critical, High, Medium, or Low.
  • 4. Spatial Deduplication: Identifies matching active tickets in the same ward and clusters them together.
  • 5. Automated Citizen WhatsApp Notification: Sends tracking receipt and target SLA commitment.
